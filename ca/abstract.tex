\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Small language models are increasingly trained by querying larger models instead of
reading datasets. When several such models sit behind priced, text-only interfaces, the
student must choose which to ask; the common default is the strongest. We study
this choice, which we term provider-selective distillation: a student with a fixed query
budget decides, block by block, which black-box provider to ask and about which domain,
while it learns from its own responses through a reward built on the provider's answers.
We build a testbed with a 1.5B-parameter student, five providers that differ in price and
quality, a corrupted copy of the strongest, and three domains with checkable answers. We
find that cheap providers teach about as fast per gradient step as expensive ones, so the
strongest and most expensive provider is the worst single teacher at an equal budget. A selector
that learns how much progress each domain yields matches the best single provider (test
utility 0.500 against 0.494) without being told which provider that is, but does not yet
beat it. Whether the best affordable provider differs by domain or changes during
training is below our current statistical resolution, and we plan the measurements
needed to settle it.
