\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ca:chap:lit}

\section{Federated distillation and LLM adaptation}

Federated Averaging trains a shared model by averaging the parameters of clients that keep
their data \autocite{mcmahan_fedavg_2017}, which requires every client to share one
architecture and disclose its weights. Federated distillation exchanges predictions
instead, distilling the averaged outputs of heterogeneous clients on public data
\autocite{li_fedmd_2019,lin_feddf_2020} and filtering unreliable ones
\autocite{shao_selectivefd_2024}. The same ideas now reach language models: FedMKT
exchanges token-level predictions across vocabularies \autocite{fan_fedmkt_2025}, FedGRPO
returns only scalar rewards from domain clients \autocite{zhu_fedgrpo_2026}, and LaDA
learns which reasoning samples each small client can absorb \autocite{guo_lada_2026}.
These methods either consult every party on every input or choose parties by their
competence on the input, and consultation is free. In contrast, our work chooses parties by
what the learner gains per unit of price.

\section{On-policy distillation and the black-box turn}

GKD and MiniLLM train the student on its own samples under a divergence to the teacher
\autocite{agarwal_gkd_2024,gu_minillm_2024}, which avoids the compounding errors of
training on the teacher's samples \autocite{ross_dagger_2011} but needs the teacher's
next-token probabilities over a shared vocabulary. Cross-tokenizer losses relax the shared
vocabulary \autocite{boizard_uld_2025,patino_gold_2025}, but any fixed alignment discards
supervision where segmentations disagree \autocite{sun_simct_2026}. Black-box methods turn
the teacher's text into a reward instead: a discriminator in GAD \autocite{ye_gad_2025},
a rubric checked by a verifier in ROPD \autocite{fang_ropd_2026}, or similarity to teacher
continuations in OmniOPD \autocite{zhou_omniopd_2026}. All of them assume one teacher queried
on every prompt. Our work keeps the black-box reward but asks which of several teachers to
query.

\section{Multi-teacher distillation}

MOPD routes each prompt to a specialist teacher for its domain
\autocite{ma_mopd_2026}, TrustMOPD weights teachers per token by a reliability proxy
\autocite{sun_trustmopd_2026}, and MAD-OPD weights them by their confidence after a debate
\autocite{wang_madopd_2026}. RL-KD learned per-instance teacher weights with reinforcement
learning, but for off-policy distillation \autocite{yuan_rlkd_2021}. In all of these the
teachers are free to query, and the routing rule is fixed by domain labels or by a property
of the teachers. In contrast, our selectors learn the rule from the student's realised
progress, under a budget.

\section{FedTOPS: progress changes what can be learned}

FedTOPS gives clients disjoint MATH prompts and one shared teacher (Qwen3-8B), runs
on-policy distillation locally and averages the adapters \autocite{chen_fedtops_2026}. It
finds that plain averaging can be no better than local training, because averaging slows a
prerequisite skill and so delays the point at which the teacher's supervision helps; its
method gains 4.6 to 14.6 points of macro accuracy over FedAvg on six mathematics
benchmarks. The mechanism is an instance of performative prediction, in which a model's own
behaviour changes the data it is next trained on \autocite{perdomo_performative_2020}, and
it is the mechanism behind our H2. FedTOPS cannot test H2, because it has one teacher. Our
work keeps its on-policy setting and its focus on the student's state, and adds several
priced, text-only teachers.

\section{Choosing sources under a budget}

Online Data Mixing treats data domains as arms of an EXP3 bandit reweighted by training
loss \autocite{albalak_odm_2023,auer_exp3_2002}, and DUET tunes a mixture across whole runs
\autocite{chen_duet_2026}; both treat sources as fixed datasets. Bandit theory supplies
budgets \autocite{badanidiyuru_bwk_2013}, drifting rewards \autocite{garivier_ducb_2011} and
context \autocite{li_linucb_2010}, and rotting bandits let an arm's reward decay as it is
pulled \autocite{levine_rotting_2017}. None of them models our case, where pulling any arm
changes the student and therefore the value of every other arm.
