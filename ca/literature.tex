\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ca:chap:lit}

This review covers the four lines of work that the project combines and states, for each,
the assumption that provider-selective distillation drops.

\section{Federated distillation and LLM adaptation}

Federated Averaging trains a shared model by averaging parameters from clients that keep
their data \autocite{mcmahan_fedavg_2017}. This requires every client to train the same
architecture and to disclose its weights. Federated distillation exchanges predictions
instead: FedMD and FedDF distil the averaged outputs of heterogeneous client models on
public data \autocite{li_fedmd_2019,lin_feddf_2020}, and Selective-FD filters unreliable
client predictions before they reach the student \autocite{shao_selectivefd_2024}.

The same ideas now reach language models. FedMKT exchanges token-level predictions between
models with different vocabularies \autocite{fan_fedmkt_2025}, and FedGRPO aggregates only
the scalar rewards that domain clients return for a server model's answers
\autocite{zhu_fedgrpo_2026}. LaDA is closest in motivation: it learns which reasoning
samples each client's small model can absorb, and finds that a larger server model does
not necessarily give a better client \autocite{guo_lada_2026}.

Two assumptions run through this work. Either every party is consulted on every input, or
parties are chosen by their competence on the input (FedGRPO), never by how much the
learner gains. And consulting a party is free apart from communication. Neither holds when
the parties are priced APIs and the question is which of them is worth paying.

\section{On-policy distillation and the black-box turn}

On-policy distillation trains the student on its own samples, with a teacher scoring the
tokens it produces. GKD and MiniLLM minimise a divergence between student and teacher on
student-generated sequences \autocite{agarwal_gkd_2024,gu_minillm_2024}, following the
imitation-learning result that training on the learner's own states avoids compounding
errors \autocite{ross_dagger_2011}. These methods need the teacher's next-token
probabilities over a vocabulary shared with the student.

Cross-tokenizer methods relax the shared vocabulary but keep logit access
\autocite{boizard_uld_2025,patino_gold_2025}. Any fixed alignment between tokenizers
discards supervision where the two segmentations disagree \autocite{sun_simct_2026}, and
the motivating experiment of Section~\ref{ca:sec:motivating} measures that loss directly.
Black-box methods drop logit access altogether and turn the teacher's text into a reward:
a learned discriminator in GAD \autocite{ye_gad_2025}, an induced rubric checked by a
verifier in ROPD \autocite{fang_ropd_2026}, or similarity to teacher continuations in
OmniOPD \autocite{zhou_omniopd_2026}. All three assume a single teacher queried on every
prompt.

\section{Multi-teacher distillation}

MOPD routes each prompt to a specialist teacher for its domain and distils on the
student's own rollouts \autocite{ma_mopd_2026}. TrustMOPD weights teachers per token by a
reliability proxy \autocite{sun_trustmopd_2026}, and MAD-OPD weights them by their
confidence after a debate \autocite{wang_madopd_2026}. In all three, teachers are
white-box, free to query, and routed by domain labels or by a property of the teachers.
RL-KD did learn per-instance teacher weights with reinforcement learning
\autocite{yuan_rlkd_2021}, but for off-policy distillation with every teacher available at
no cost. None of these chooses teachers by what the student gains per unit of cost.

\section{FedTOPS: progress changes what can be learned}

FedTOPS gives clients disjoint prompt sets and a single shared white-box teacher
(Qwen3-8B), runs on-policy distillation locally, and averages the adapters
\autocite{chen_fedtops_2026}. It finds that plain averaging can be no better than local
training, because averaging slows a prerequisite skill and so delays the student's arrival
at states where the teacher's supervision helps. Its method uses the teacher's agreement
with the averaged model to scale the server update, and gains 4.6 to 14.6 points of macro
accuracy over FedAvg on six mathematics benchmarks.

The mechanism is an instance of performative prediction, where a model's own behaviour
changes the data it is next trained on \autocite{perdomo_performative_2020}. It is also
the mechanism behind H2. FedTOPS cannot test H2, because it has one teacher, logit access
and no prices. This project keeps its on-policy setting and its focus on the student's
state, and changes the other three.

\section{Choosing sources under a budget}

Online Data Mixing treats data domains as arms of an EXP3 bandit and reweights them by
training loss \autocite{albalak_odm_2023,auer_exp3_2002}, and DUET tunes a mixture across
whole training runs \autocite{chen_duet_2026}. Both treat sources as fixed datasets whose
only cost is compute. Bandit theory supplies the remaining pieces: budgets
\autocite{badanidiyuru_bwk_2013}, drifting rewards \autocite{garivier_ducb_2011} and
context \autocite{li_linucb_2010}. Rotting bandits model an arm whose reward decays as it
is pulled \autocite{levine_rotting_2017}. None of them models the case here, where pulling
any arm changes the student and with it the value of every other arm.
